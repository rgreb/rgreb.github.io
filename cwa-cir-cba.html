Section 1: Bargaining Unit Titles

All employees covered by this Agreement shall be defined as the “bargaining unit”, which includes hourly employees but excludes all supervisors, managers, or confidential employees.

Whenever the employer seeks to create a new bargaining unit title, it shall notify the union as soon as practicable and the parties shall meet to negotiate the category designation and hourly rate or annual salary for the new title.

Section 2: Successorship Agreement

In the event that the employer makes an agreement to merge or affiliate with another entity and such agreement may impact the recognition of the union and/or the terms and conditions of employment of the employees, then such agreement will guarantee the continued recognition of the union and the continuation of all terms and conditions of employment (at least comparable) as contained in this Agreement.

ARTICLE II: UNION SECURITY AND DUES CHECKOFF

Section 1:

All employees covered by this Agreement shall, as a condition of employment, either:

A. Become and remain members of the union in good standing during the term of their employment, it being understood that membership in good standing on the part of an employee shall mean the tender by such employee of the periodic dues and assessments uniformly required as a condition of employment, or

B. Pay an agency shop fee to the union equal to the dues uniformly required as a condition of employment.

Section 2:

No discrimination or reprisal shall be made against any employee by either party to this contract because of membership or non-membership in the union.

Section 3:

The union shall have the exclusive right to the checkoff and transmittal of dues or agency shop fees on behalf of each employee in the bargaining unit, said dues or fees to be checked off biweekly from the paycheck of each employee pursuant to the direction of the union, in such amounts as the union shall establish.

Section 4:

It is specifically agreed that the Employer assumes no obligation, financial or otherwise, arising out of the provisions of this Article, and the Union hereby agrees that it will indemnify and hold the Employer harmless from any claims, actions or proceedings, including attorney’s fees and court costs, by any employee arising from deductions made by the Employer pursuant to this Article. Once the funds are remitted to the Union, their disposition shall be the sole and exclusive obligation and responsibility of the Union.

Section 5: Longevity Differential

On their third anniversary of hire and on each subsequent anniversary of hire, the salary rate of each employee shall be modified by adding an amount equal to 0.5% (one-half of one percent) of the job rate (e.g., an employee on their sixth anniversary of hire shall have amount equal to 2% [two percent] of the job rate added to their salary).

Section 6: Differentials

Each Senior Paralegal shall receive a differential of $2,470 per calendar year.
Each Senior Legal Assistant shall receive a differential of $1,000 per calendar year.
Each Senior Dues and Data Associate shall receive a differential of $1,000 per calendar year.
Each Lead Dues and Data Associate shall receive a differential of $1,000 per calendar year.

Section 7: Severance Pay

Severance pay will be paid to an employee for the following reasons:
Job Elimination
- In the event an employee turns down a transfer to another state and elects to leave their position.
- No longer being able to work based on a permanent disability.

Severance pay shall be as follows:
- An employee employed for two (2) years or more shall receive two (2) weeks of severance pay.
- An employee shall receive an additional 1/2 week for each year employed, up to a maximum of ten (10) weeks.

Employee Resignation:

  • Any employee who has been employed for five (5) years but less than ten (10) years and resigns from the employer in good standing will receive a payout for unused sick leave up to a maximum of eight (8) sick days for the calendar year in which the employee resigns.
  • Any employee who has been employed for ten (10) years or more and resigns from the employer in good standing will receive a payout for unused sick leave up to a maximum of twelve (12) sick days for the calendar year in which the employee resigns.

ARTICLE IV: HOURS

Section 1: Hourly Employees

A. The basic work week for Category 1-5 employees shall be seven (7) hours daily, Monday-Friday or a total of thirty-five (35) hours per week. When an employee works approved overtime, they shall be paid at their regular straight time hourly rate of pay for each hour worked in excess of thirty-five (35) and up to thirty-seven and one-half (37 1/2) hours, and at the rate of one and one-half (1.5) times his/her regular straight time hourly rate for each hour worked in excess of thirty-seven and one-half (37 1/2) hours. Overtime worked on Saturdays shall be paid at the rate of one and one-half times the employee’s regular straight time hourly rate. Overtime worked on Sundays and paid holidays shall be paid at the rate of two (2) times the employee’s regular straight time hourly rate. The regular straight time hourly rate shall be computed by dividing the employee’s annual salary by 1,820 hours.

B. Flextime Schedules

The employer may consider specific requests for flextime schedules. Such schedules may range from 8:00 AM to 7:00 PM. Similarly, the employer may consider summer hour schedules respecting individual requests and office needs.

C. Overtime Scheduling

The employer may schedule overtime for office personnel if one week’s notice is given. If proper notice is not given, the employees may not be required to work overtime except in the case of an emergency.

D. Fair Allocation of Overtime

The employer understands the employees have an interest in the fair allocation of overtime work and will endeavor to make such allocations based on the individual skills and availability of employees and the needs of the organization. The union understands that overtime work may be required of all employees from time to time. If the union feels that overtime is not distributed fairly, the employer will meet with the union to address any overtime distribution issues. Refusal to work overtime shall be counted as hours worked in assessing fair distribution.

Section 2: Salaried Employees

Due to the irregular and demanding nature of our work, and the desire to maintain a reasonable work/life balance for all employees, CIR endorses the principle of flexible scheduling for salaried employees. Both parties recognize that the implementation of flextime cannot prevent or interfere with the accomplishment of the mission, goals and tasks of CIR, nor impede in the professional development of its staff.

When a salaried employee is working in the late evening, early morning, or on weekends, they may request flexible scheduling for a future date.

Requests for flexible scheduling can be made verbally or in writing. If the requested schedule is denied for reasons of organizational necessity, scheduling, or professional development, the supervisor will make an effort to accommodate the request at a later time.

Flexible scheduling is not intended to replace compensatory time and thus cannot be requested in increments larger than a one-half work day. Flexible scheduling will not be granted on a one hour to one hour basis for additional hours worked.

Section 3: Compensatory Time for Salaried Employees

At the discretion of an employee’s supervisor, salaried employees will be given:

A)

One (1) full day off for each full weekend day worked of seven (7) or more hours, and one-half (1/2) day off, or three and one-half (3.5) hours off, for working three and one-half to seven (3.5-7) hours on a weekend day.

B)

One (1) full day off for each weekend day of CIR convention worked for more than three and one-half (3.5) hours.

The compensatory full days and one-half days can only be taken after the employee has requested the time off in writing, and it has been approved by their supervisor. Section 4: Work from Home

The Employer and Union agree that ensuring the health and safety of staff is a priority. All staff when not assigned field duties may be allowed to work from home. In the event that office based work is necessary, each Department Manager in consultation with the Executive Director and Associate Director, will determine the schedule that best completes the work needed, and fosters a positive team atmosphere for CIR. Managers may implement any of the following weekly schedules for their department:

  1. Five days in the Office
  2. One day at home and four days in the office
  3. Two days at home and three days in the office
  4. Three days at home and two days in the office
  5. Four days at home and one day in the office
  6. Five days at home and zero in the office

CIR will provide advance notice of five working days in the event that the office-based work schedule is changed. However, once a schedule is set, it must stay fixed for at least 10 days before it can be changed again, except for emergency or unforeseen special circumstances. CIR will provide all equipment and supplies necessary to complete job functions. CIR agrees to discuss on an ongoing basis the schedule that best fits each department’s needs during the monthly labor management meetings when requested by the CWA. The final determination of the work schedule will remain at the discretion of management; however, CIR agrees to not exercise this scheduling discretion in an unfair, arbitrary, or capricious manner.

A. Digital Surveillance

CIR will not use surveillance technology such as keystroke monitoring software or eye movement tracking software for staff who are working from home.

B. Healthy & Safety In the Office

CIR will implement the following health and safety protocols during a national or state Government declared emergency related to a viral illness:

  • Provide proper PPE to prevent exposure.
  • Provide hand sanitizer.
  • To the extent practicable, allow staff to maintain 6 feet of social distancing in shared or open layout spaces, or add barriers in between work spaces.
  • CIR and CWA will establish an Office Health & Safety Subcommittee, to establish proper safety protocols specific to the viral illness, made up of three bargaining.

Unit Persons and Benefit Managers

  1. For the COVID-19 national emergency, CIR will implement the following health and safety protocols:
    • CIR will provide one N95 mask per worker per day that they are required to be present in the office.
    • CIR will ensure a clean and sanitized workspace by having the office professionally cleaned at least three times weekly and providing cleaning supplies to staff as needed.
    • CIR will maintain an entrance protocol for the Office requiring staff to take their temperature and record it on a log sheet with the following questions to monitor for symptoms:
      • Do you have symptoms of COVID-19?
      • Have you tested positive for COVID-19 within the past five days?
      • Have you been exposed to COVID-19 and are waiting for a test result/have not yet taken a test?
      • Do you have a temperature of 100.4 °F or higher?
        • If a staff member answers yes to any of the above questions, they will not be permitted to enter the Office.
        • CIR and CWA agree to discuss the appropriate staff limit to be in the office at any one time, on an ongoing basis in the labor management meetings based on pandemic conditions.
        • In the case that a full department needs to be present in the Office, staff shall be given the option to work in other areas in the office to ensure that they are able to keep social distance.
    • In the event that an individual was present in the office who has a confirmed or suspected diagnosis of COVID-19, the Office will be closed until it can be cleaned and sanitized. Any other staff present in the Office at the same time as the infected individual shall be immediately notified and instructed to follow the CDC guidelines in effect at that time.

Health and Safety for All Staff

  • It will be a condition of employment for all CIR staff to be fully vaccinated against the COVID virus. Staff may seek exemptions for bona fide religious reasons or for documented medical reasons preventing such vaccination. In such cases in which an exemption shall apply, CIR shall make reasonable accommodations that do not pose an undue hardship on CIR. The meaning of “fully vaccinated” shall be that: A person must have received all injections based on the vaccine manufacturer instructions (Pfizer/Moderna: 2 shots or J&J: 1 shot) AND any booster shots as recommended by the CDC.

ARTICLE V: EMPLOYEES

Section 1: Categories of Employees

Regular Employees

  1. Full-time, hourly employees: non-exempt employees, regularly scheduled to work at least thirty-five (35) hours per week.
  2. Full-time, salaried employees: exempt employees, regularly working at least four (4) days per week.
  3. Part-time, hourly employees: non-exempt employees, regularly scheduled to work fewer than thirty-five (35) hours per week.
  4. Part-time, salaried employees: exempt employees, regularly working less than four (4) days per week.

Temporary Employees

  • Project Staff Employees: employees who are hired to work for an assigned project.
  • Seasonal Employees: employees who are hired to work three (3) months or less.

The employer agrees to comply with all applicable Federal, State and Local laws and regulations, including but not limited to, the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) regarding the classification of employees.

Section 2: Seasonal and Project Staff Employees

Seasonal employees:

  • May be continuously employed for up to three (3) months.
  • Upon expiration of the three (3) month period, the employer shall exercise one of the following options: Offer regular employment to the seasonal employee; or notify the employee that their temporary employment will end.
  • Employees who are made a regular employee shall have their probationary period begin effective the date of original hire.

Project Staff Employees:

  • Full-and part-time project staff employees may be hired for a period of up to one year.
  • Such project staff employees shall not be covered by Article VI: Probation, Article VII: Layoff, and Article XIV: Grievance Procedure of this agreement.
  • Upon completion of the project or the period of hire, the employer shall exercise one of the options listed above for seasonal employees.
  • Project staff employees offered regular employment will not be required to have a probationary period where their responsibilities and required skills will be the same as in their project positions.
  • A written description of the project, necessity, and timeline for a Project Staff position must be submitted to the union.

Necessity and whether a part-time position is intended to be a regular part-time position must be communicated to the union prior to a position being posted.

Section 3: No Undermining of the Bargaining Unit

It is not the intention of the employer to undermine the bargaining unit by contracting out or hiring consultants and seasonal employees.

Section 4: Category 7 Employees

CIR agrees that identifying, developing, and retaining staff leadership is a key priority of the union. Toward that end, a Category 7 staff position was created. This title encompasses four broad categories of responsibility: Leadership, training, strategic planning, and coordination. A Category 7 employee will be those individuals taking on additional responsibilities as a part of the leadership of an area, or significant campaign and will possess the ability to be put in charge of other assigned staff in the area or campaign. In addition, the Category 7 person will have the ability to create and follow through on a strategic work plan independently and lead others in completion of the strategic, internal organizing, external organizing, or political campaign(s). Correspondent with these responsibilities, Category 7 employees are granted increased work autonomy as well as educational benefits.

A. Application Process for Category 7

Available Category 7 positions will be posted to all staff. Application to a Category 7 position will be made in writing, and selection will be determined by a panel of three administrative staff. This panel will take into account the following factors in their decision: evaluations, feedback from other CIR staff, and an interview with three members of the panel. The panel will notify the applicant of their decision in writing. If denied, the panel will highlight areas of improvement for the applicant, and recommend a timeframe for re-application.

B. Promotion Request for Category 7

A Category 6 employee, who has been employed by CIR as a Category 6 employee for at least two (2) years, and who meets the criteria outlined by the Article V Category 7 description, and who has been assigned the duties of a Category 7 role during a major campaign(s) may request to be promoted to Category 7. The request shall be in writing to their Department Director. Within forty-five (45) days, the employee’s Department Director and the Executive Director will meet with the employee to discuss their request. After the meeting, the Executive Director and Department Director will determine if the work needed by CIR and the performance of the employee meets the criteria of promotion to a Category 7 level for the employee requesting consideration. The Department Director and the Executive Director will notify the employee in writing of their decision. If denied, they will highlight areas of improvement, and if the work required by CIR would be at a Category 7 level in the foreseeable future recommend a timeframe to possibly make the request again.

C. Trial Period for Category 7 Lead Positions

For staff promoted to Category 7 Lead Positions within their existing region or department (Lead Contract Organizer, Lead New Organizer, Lead Coordinator) as an internal hire will spend twelve (12) months in a “trial period.” During this 12-month trial period, should a Category 7 employee not be meeting the expectations of their position, the immediate supervisor will call a meeting to issue a written warning, discuss the performance issues and create a written plan.

D. Promotion of Internal Hires to Category 7 Positions Outside of Their Region or Department

Category 7 internal hires who are promoted to a position that is not within their Department or Region shall not be subject to a probation or trial period. Such internal hires shall instead be subject to Article XV, Disciplinary Procedures for any disciplinary actions taken against them.

E. Severance Pay

In the event that a Category 7 employee opts out of Category 7, and declines the option to move back to Category 6 and chooses to leave CIR, severance will be paid as follows:

  • An employee who has been employed for one (1) year but less than five (5) years and leaves in good standing can receive a payout for unused sick leave to a maximum of five (5) days for the calendar year in which the employee resigns.

  • An employee who has been employed for five (5) years but less than ten (10) years and leaves in good standing can receive a payout for unused sick leave to a maximum of eight (8) days for the calendar year in which the employee resigns.

  • An employee who has been employed for ten (10) years or more and resigns from the employer in good standing can receive a payout for unused sick leave to a maximum of ten (10) days for the calendar year in which the employee resigns.

F. Flexible Scheduling

It is the expectation that Category 7 staff will schedule their own work and keep their supervisor apprised of this schedule. Changes to this schedule will only be mandated by organizational necessity.

G. Probationary Period for External New Hires

ARTICLE VI: PROBATION

Section 1: Category 1-5 Probation

The probationary period for all new employees hired in Categories 1-5 shall be for a period of up to four (4) months from the date of hire and shall include time worked in a seasonal capacity. The supervisor will meet with the employee to discuss their performance one (1) month and two (2) months from the employee’s date of hire. The employee will receive a formal written probationary evaluation from their supervisor.

Section 2: Category 6 Probation

The probationary period for all new employees hired in Category 6 shall be for a period of up to six (6) months. The supervisor will meet with the employee to discuss their performance within thirty to forty (30-40) calendar days from their date of hire. The employee will receive a formal written probationary evaluation from their supervisor within ninety to one hundred (90-100) calendar days from their date of hire and a final formal written probationary evaluation.

Probationary evaluations are evaluations within the meaning of Article XXIII of this Agreement.

Section 3: Expiration of Probationary Period

At least ten (10) days prior to the expiration of the probationary period, the employer shall exercise one of the following options:
- Offer regular employment to the employee
- Extend the probationary period for up to:
- An additional two (2) month period for employees in Categories 1-5, OR
- An additional three (3) month period, for employees in Category 6
- Terminate the employee’s employment with at least two (2) weeks’ notice.

Section 4: Extension of Probation

If there is an extension of the probationary period, the supervisor will provide the employee with a formal written evaluation for every month, at a minimum, the probationary period is extended. The evaluation shall inform the employee that they have passed probation, failed probation, or that the employer needs more time to evaluate the employee. If the employer fails to evaluate the employee, the employee may request an evaluation in writing. The employer will respond to this request by conducting an evaluation within two (2) weeks.

Section 5: Employer Rights

At any time during the probationary period or any extension thereof, the employer may discipline, discharge, demote, transfer or lay off the employee without regard to the provisions of Article XV: Disciplinary procedure, or Article VII: Layoff and any such discipline, discharge, demote, transfer or layoff shall not be subject to the grievance and arbitration provisions contained in Article XIV: Grievance Procedure or Article XV: Disciplinary Procedure with the sole exception of a grievance alleging discrimination or failure to adhere to Section 1 or 2 above. The employer shall not be required, as a remedy in a grievance or arbitration alleging a failure to adhere to Section 1 or 2 above, to offer regular employment or extend a probationary period for longer than four (4) weeks.

Section 6: Offer Letter

When hired the new employee shall be given a letter that states their start date, that the employee will be evaluated during his/her probationary period, and the date that probation ends.

Section 7: Meeting with Staff Union

The employer will inform the union of all newly hired employees, who are members of the union. The union will be allowed to schedule a thirty (30) minute meeting with the new employee within one (1) week of the employee’s start date.

Section 8: Purpose of Probationary Period

The employer and the union agree that the Probation Period shall be used to train, orient and evaluate new employees. The supervisor will advise the employee of the particular requirements for the job and will use this opportunity to assess the employee’s job knowledge, their approach to work, their work habits and initiative, and their interaction with co-workers and CIR members.

Section 9: Additional Training

CIR recognizes that new employees are hired into Category 6 positions with varying amounts of experience. For employees with limited experience, additional training opportunities and supervision are key to their professional development and success.

Therefore, employees hired at Steps 1 through 4 will receive additional formal feedback, after completing the probationary period, focused on their development on a quarterly basis until reaching Step 5. To facilitate their professional development those employees hired at Steps 1 through 4 will receive additional training and mentorship, based on the supervisor’s assessment of the employee’s needs.

ARTICLE VII: LAYOFF

Section 1: Seniority

Seniority shall be defined as follows: - Seniority is accrued only during regular status, including probation. - Seniority is continuous time worked. - Part-time employees accrue pro-rated seniority. - Seniority shall be broken by a termination for cause or resignation. - A leave of absence or reduction in force does not constitute a break in service. - Seniority shall not continue to accrue during a layoff. Seniority shall continue to accrue during any paid leave of absence.

Section 2: Reduction in Force (RIF)

In the event of a necessary reduction in force (RIF), the following policies shall govern layoffs: - The employer shall identify the job categories and number of full-time employees necessary in each category.

  • If there is a layoff in a given category, the layoff shall occur in the following order:
    • First, all seasonal employees;
    • Second, project staff employees; and
    • Third, regular full-time and part-time employees on the combined basis of seniority and ability to do the remaining work.

Whenever possible, the employer and the union will try to identify all employment opportunities and to avoid layoffs by transferring, reassigning or offering to demote employees to available vacancies. Any vacancies accepted as a result of the layoff that required location to another state shall be governed by Article IX, Section 5.

Section 3: Notice and Voluntary Layoff

The Union shall be given at least two (2) months’ notice before any layoff. The Employer shall, at the time notice is provided, present the Union with copies of the budget and financial projections it relied upon when it made the decision to do layoffs. Before any mandatory layoff, the parties shall meet to discuss a voluntary layoff plan consisting of layoffs outside of the procedure above and/or voluntary shared time agreements. The employer will not unreasonably deny a voluntary layoff plan proposed by the union.

Section 4: Insurance Benefits

The employer will continue to provide insurance benefits to regular employees on layoff based on years of service:

  • If an employee has at least one (1) year of service but less than five (5) years of service, the employee will receive up to eight (8) weeks of HSBP and VHHSBP benefits.
  • If an employee has between five (5) years of service but less than ten (10) years of service the employee will receive up to seventeen (17) weeks of HSBP and VHHSBP benefits.
  • If an employee has between ten (10) years of service but less than fifteen (15) years of service the employee will receive up to thirty (30) weeks of HSBP and VHHSBP benefits.
  • If an employee has more than fifteen (15) years of service the employee will receive up to forty-three (43) weeks of HSBP and VHHSBP benefits.

Coverage will be given provided that the employee has no other employment or equivalent benefits. The employer will have the right to coordinate benefits as the secondary carrier.

Section 5: Recall

Laid-off employees shall have recall rights as follows:

  • If an employee has five (5) years or less of service, the employee will have six (6) months of recall rights.
  • If an employee has more than five (5) years but less than ten (10) years of service, the employee will have one (1) year of recall rights.
  • If an employee has ten (10) years of service but less than fifteen (15) years of service, the employee will have eighteen (18) months of recall rights.
  • If an employee has fifteen (15) years or more of service, the employee will have two (2) years of recall rights.

The order for recall shall be inverse to the layoff, in that the last laid-off shall be the first recalled.

ARTICLE VIII: JOB DESCRIPTIONS

Section 1: Job descriptions for new employees

All new employees will receive a written job description that their direct supervisor will review with them within two (2) weeks of their start date.

Section 2: Creation of a new job description/Amending a current job description

The employer shall have the right to create a job description for a new position for purposes of posting that job prior to the Union and the employer meeting to discuss that job description. Job descriptions shall be provided to the union and shall be made available upon request. If the employer seeks to create a new job description the union shall be provided written notice. The parties shall schedule a labor/management meeting for the purpose of discussing the description(s) in question within fourteen (14) days of notice.

If the employer and the Union do not agree on the new job description within fourteen (14) days from the time the job description was discussed with the Union, the employer has the right to implement said job description to be posted.

If the employer seeks to amend an existing job description, the union shall be provided seven (7) days written notice. Adjustments to any aspect of the job description including but not limited to job summary, job responsibilities, education requirements, qualifications, years of experience, and salary (per the CBA increases) will be highlighted. The employer will be open for discussion with the union on subjects such as job summary and job responsibilities. The employer may post the mandated job description seven (7) days after providing the union written notice.

Section 3: Job Description Posting

For job descriptions with no changes or amended job descriptions, the employer will provide one full day’s (24 hours) written notice to the Union for the purpose of posting internally. All internal candidates who meet the job’s qualifications will be given an interview and priority consideration for the open position.

Section 4: Storage and access to job descriptions

The employer will store and maintain in a designated location, accessible to all employees, all job descriptions for currently held positions. Each job description will be a separate file, named with the common naming convention: Title of Job, Department, Region, Date Updated.

ARTICLE IX: Out-of-Title Work and Change in Work Assignment

Section 1: Out-of-Title Work Definition

Work is “out-of-title” when it is not within the employee’s job description and is not incidental to such work. Out-of-title work may be assigned to an employee where, in the employer’s judgment, the needs of the organization so require. If the salary of the job being performed is higher, the employee shall receive the upgrade in their salary rate for the duration of the time in excess of two (2) days they perform the work.

Section 2: Change in Work Assignment

Where through a change in work assignment the requirements, description or responsibilities of a job change, the supervisor of record will schedule a meeting with the employee to discuss any new duties, job description or change of responsibility. Where there is no agreement between the parties, the matter may be submitted to binding arbitration under the American Arbitration Association to the date of change, provided that questions of unit clarification will be resolved by application to the NLRB instead of arbitration.

Section 3: Out of Town Assignments

  1. When field-based employees are required to be out of town, the employer shall provide transportation back to the employee’s regularly assigned area or reimburse the employee for an individual of their choice to travel to the employee at their out of town assignment at least every two (2) weeks.

  2. For field organizers on an away assignment for more than one (1) month the employee will have the option to be reimbursed for a roundtrip ticket to their regularly assigned area or home base for the weekend, unless weekend work is required by the campaign, when the cost of keeping the employee in the area is higher than the cost of a roundtrip ticket. Travel costs may not exceed those the employer would have incurred on behalf of the employee and that the employee shall remain fully responsible for submitting and verifying such expenses. Expenses will be paid in accordance with the Expense Reimbursement Policy.

3. Additional Assignments

When an employee, while still covering their current assignment, is assigned to cover a vacated position during the hiring process or while covering for an employee’s extended absence due to parental, disability, or other family medical leaves lasting two weeks or more, the employee will be paid a weekly salary bonus of two-hundred and fifty dollars ($250) per week for the length of the assignment, with payment beginning the second week of the assignment, retroactive to the first day of the assignment.

CIR will make a good faith effort to not assign employees remotely in a different time zone other than their normally assigned time zone, unless it’s their only assignment.

Section 4: Notification of Assignment

Any employee that is reassigned must receive at least one (1) week’s advance notification (barring extenuating circumstances). Such notification should indicate if the reassignment is permanent or temporary. If temporary, the notification should include an assignment length not to exceed six (6) months.

Section 5: Employee Transfers

A current employee who transfers to a new job, department, or region will meet with their new supervisor to discuss their performance one (1) month and three (3) months from the date of transfer. The employee will receive a formal written evaluation from their supervisor six (6) months from their date of transfer. All subsequent annual evaluations will be based on the date of transfer.

If a current employee transfers and receives a salary increase outside of the schedule in Article 3: Wages; Section 4, the date of the salary increase will be used as the ‘Step Hired At’ for determining all future salary step increases.

An employee who, in place of a layoff, accepts a transfer, reassignment, or demotion to another Region shall be reimbursed up to $5,000 for transportation expenses and other expenses reasonably related to the cost of moving the employee and their family, provided they submit verifiable receipts. There shall be no reduction in seniority, salary, or other benefits as a result of the transfer.

CIR will only reimburse moving expenses that qualify as tax deductible under IRS guidelines.

Section 6: Per Diem

Employees participating in authorized business travel consistent with CIR policy shall receive per diem rates as defined by the IRS. Employees shall not receive a per diem or be otherwise reimbursed for meals if said meals are provided by CIR or a third-party. Employees on an away assignment who are required to be at the out-of-town location on a weekend they are not required to work are eligible to receive the defined per diem rate for the weekend day/s stayed in the destination city.

ARTICLE X: LEAVE TIME

Section 1: Vacation

A. Newly hired, regular full-time employees begin accruing vacation the first day of the month following their start date. Each month the employee will accrue vacation at 1.66 days per month. Employees shall be credited their full vacation allotment at the beginning of each calendar year in anticipation of continued employment. All vacation accrual is subject to the conditions set forth in Section 9 of this Article.

B. Regular, full-time employees shall also accrue one (1) additional day of vacation for each year of employment after five (5) years until reaching a maximum of five (5) weeks of vacation after completing ten (10) years of service.

C. Regular part-time employees shall receive pro-rated entitlement to holidays, sick leave, and vacation.

D. Seasonal employees shall not receive vacation. Seasonal employees who are given a regular position will be entitled to vacation in their first year of employment following the new hire vacation entitlement effective upon their employment date as a regular employee.

E. Project Staff employees will earn vacation after three (3) months of employment. Vacation will be given in the same manner as a new employee.

F. Vacation time may only be taken in increments of one-half (1/2) day or a full day.

G. Anyone hired between October through December in any given calendar year will receive 1.66 days of vacation per month which can be used through March 31st of the following year.

H. In the event the employer requires an employee to either cancel a vacation or not take unused vacation, the employee will be able to carry over that unused vacation into the following year or get paid for that unused vacation at the discretion of the employer. The unused vacation carried over must be used no later than March 1. In order to get paid for unused vacation, the employee must receive authorization from the Executive Director or designee.

I. Vacation rollover. All employees are not limited in the number of vacation days that they may roll over into the next calendar year. However, employees may only carry up to 1.5 times their annual amount of accrued vacation time at any time in a given year. This includes any earned vacation days based on years of service. Once an employee reaches this amount, they will cease accruing additional vacation time until their total amount of unused vacation is less than this amount. An employee who has made multiple attempts to schedule vacation, but has had those requests denied prior to reaching the cap of 1.5 times their vacation limit and has notified Human Resources each time vacation is denied prior to reaching the cap, their annual amount of accrued vacation time shall continue to accrue above their cap.

J. Vacation requests submitted in writing during January 1 - March 31 inclusive, shall be granted in order of seniority for that calendar year. Thereafter, vacation requests in writing shall be approved on a first come basis. The employer will reply in writing to a vacation request within two (2) weeks of the request.

K. Upon voluntary or involuntary termination of employment, an employee shall be entitled to payment for unused vacation time accrued for the year of termination as follows: Total vacation days x number of pay periods past divided by 26 minus vacation days used.

Section 3: Sick Leave

A. Each employee shall be entitled to twelve (12) days of sick leave with pay per year. Each newly hired employee will receive one (1) sick day per month beginning in the first month following their date of hire.

B. Unused sick days will carry over for use in the following year and each year thereafter, except that no employee may accumulate more than forty-five (45) days.

C. Regular part-time staff shall receive a pro-rated number of sick days with pay.

D. Project staff will earn one (1) sick day per quarter. Seasonal staff shall receive a pro-rated number of sick days with pay after thirty (30) days of employment.

E. Sick days can be used for family illness and can be taken in one-quarter (1/4) day, one-half (1/2) day and whole day increments for hourly employees and in one-half (1/2) day or whole day increments for salaried employees. Variation in working hours may be arranged to accommodate doctor appointments when feasible at the discretion of the employer.

F. In or about January of each calendar year, employees in categories 1-5 and in the position of Paralegal or Senior Paralegal shall elect either (1) to carry over up to twelve (12) unused sick days to the following year or (2) to receive a payment for up to twelve (12) unused sick leave from the previous calendar year.

Section 4: Family and Medical Leave and Parental Leave Benefit

Family and Medical Leave

A. Eligible employees are entitled to up to twelve (12) weeks of unpaid leave, in accordance with the Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA).

B. If an employee is covered by a policy, agreement or applicable law that provides a greater family and medical leave benefit, it supersedes the FMLA.

C. For purposes of this section, a certified domestic partner shall be considered an immediate family member.

D. All periods of paid leave pursuant to this Agreement, including sick leave shall run concurrently with any Family and Medical Leave.

E. Employer-paid health benefits will continue during Family and Medical leave.

F. Seniority will continue to accrue during Family and Medical leave.

G. Vacation, sick and personal time will not accrue during any period of unpaid FMLA leave. Employees are not eligible for holiday pay during any unpaid FMLA leave.

Parental Leave Benefit

CIR is committed to supporting employees who become parents through the CIR Parental Leave Benefit. This benefit provides eligible employees with up to twelve (12) full weeks of parental leave with pay and up to 14 additional weeks of time off for activities related to the care and well-being of a new child during the first 12 months.

Parental Leave

Eligible employees shall be provided with up to twelve (12) weeks of Parental Leave with full pay. Parental Leave may be taken intermittently.

Employees must meet FMLA employment criteria in order to be eligible for CIR’s Parental Leave Benefit. CIR’s Parental Leave Benefit runs concurrently with FMLA. Employees may access the Parental Leave Benefit for any of the following qualifying events: 1. Giving birth to a child, 2. Caring for the employee’s newly born child, or 3. Placement of a child with the employee for adoption or foster care.

The terms and eligibility of such leave shall be determined by state law. An eligible employee whose paid leave benefits will be provided by CIR’s insurance carrier must submit their application to the carrier and comply with applicable certification requirements. CIR will complete the employer portion of the application. Once an employee’s application for paid leave is approved by the carrier or appropriate state agency, benefits shall be paid by the carrier or state agency. In accordance with the appropriate state laws, paid family leave benefits are funded through payroll deductions at a prescribed amount from each eligible employee.

Employees may supplement paid family leave with accrued paid time off (sick, vacation, personal leave) in order to receive full pay during a paid family leave absence, but employees are not required to do so. If an employee chooses to apply paid time off, it may be used in half day increments, to the extent allowed by law. While on paid family leave, employees will not continue to accrue sick or vacation days.

Employees must meet FMLA employment criteria in order to be eligible for CIR’s Parental Leave Benefit. CIR’s Parental Leave Benefit runs concurrently with FMLA. Employees may access the Parental Leave Benefit for any of the following qualifying events: 1. Giving birth to a child, 2. Caring for the employee’s newly born child, or 3. Placement of a child with the employee for adoption or foster care.

An employee must be in the employ of the employer for at least forty-five (45) days to receive a personal day. Personal days are prorated from the date of hire for the balance of the calendar year.

Time spent on paid vacation, sick, or personal days can be counted toward an employee’s eligibility determination.

F. Notice Requirements

An employee must provide CIR at least 30 days’ notice before leave is to begin if the qualifying reason for leave is foreseeable. When not foreseeable, an employee must provide notice as soon as practicable.

H. Intermittent PFL Notice

Employees taking PFL on an intermittent basis must provide notice as soon as practicable before each day of intermittent leave.

Section 7: Bereavement Leave

In the event of the death of a family member, or of a chosen family member, employees may take up to five (5) working days bereavement leave. Employees may take Bereavement leave from the date of passing of their loved one, through sixty days after the funeral service. An employee can use their earned sick time, vacation, or other paid leave time for additional bereavement time if the employee is not ready to return to work after the initial bereavement leave(s). When making the request for bereavement leave(s), employees will notify CIR of the days needed and their relationship to the loved one who passed. The employee will notify their supervisor of the dates needed for bereavement leave(s) as soon as possible and will keep their supervisor updated if there is a need for additional bereavement time off from work.

Section 8: Leave for Jury Duty

All employees who are called for and serve jury duty shall be paid their regular straight time pay for the first three (3) days of jury duty; thereafter the employer will pay the difference between the per diem jury duty and the employee’s regular straight time pay for the day. In the event that an employee works in a state whereby the State’s Jury Duty leave policy offers a higher level of benefit, that benefit will apply. To be entitled to jury duty pay, the employee must present the employer with written evidence of jury duty serviced and a copy of the receipt for payment of jury duty.

Section 9: Employee Authorization of Time Off Accrual

All employees are required to choose one of the following, in writing:

Option #1:

Accept full vacation, sick and personal day allotment at the beginning of each calendar year in anticipation of continued employment, and agree that: - In the event the employee takes an authorized leave of absence, they will have any vacation, sick and/or personal time used in excess of what they accrued deducted from their full annual allotment for the following year, AND - In the event the employee terminates employment, they authorize the employer to deduct from their paycheck any vacation, sick and/or personal days used in excess of vacation, sick and personal days accrued.

Option #2:

Agree to utilize vacation, sick and personal days that the employee has accrued to date.

If an employee does not select one of the options in writing, they will be approved for vacation, sick and personal time based on accrued time to date.

Employees can change their selection in writing. The change will go into effect the first day of the month following receipt of the written notification.

Section 10 - Summer Floating Days

A. Employees shall receive two (2) additional summer floating days off. These days must be taken between July 1 and Labor Day.

B. Approval must be given in advance from the employee’s supervisor in order to use the two floating days.

ARTICLE XI: HEALTH AND WELFARE BENEFITS

Section 1: Regular Full Time Employees

CIR shall continue as a participating employer in the House Staff Benefits Plan (HSBP) and Voluntary Hospitals House Staff Benefits Plan (VHHSBP), pursuant to the rules and regulations of the Plans and in accordance with applicable law. All full-time, regular employees shall be enrolled in the plans, including coverage of dependents and spousal equivalents. For purposes of this section, an employee who works 80% of full time (i.e. 4 days = 80%) shall be considered full-time. Except for timely payments of contributions, CIR assumes no responsibility for health and welfare benefits provided through HSBP and VHHSBP; such benefits shall be governed by the rules and regulations of the Plans, the terms of any insurance contracts, and applicable law. In addition to the benefits provided through HSBP and VH-HSBP, CIR shall reimburse covered employees for the cost of any major medical deductible.

Section 2: Seasonal Employees and Regular Part Time Employees (less than 80%)

On the first day of the calendar month following the employee’s start date, employees who work more than 50% of full time will receive single core Health Benefits (no supplemental!) with the option to pay the difference in cost for full family coverage and/or supplemental coverage.

After six months, employees that work more than 50% of full time will receive family core benefits with the option to pay the difference for supplemental coverage.

After 12 months, seasonal employees that work 80% of full time (4 days) will receive full benefits as defined in Article XI: Benefits.

Section 3: Supplemental Short-Term Disability Benefit

CIR will supplement benefit payments made under the short-term disability coverage so that regular employees who have been employed at CIR from one to two years shall receive 50% of their salary, and so that regular employees who have been employed by CIR from two (2) to three (3) years shall receive 80% of their salary, and so that regular employees who have been employed by CIR for three (3) years or more shall receive 90% of their salary, for the entire period (12 weeks) covered under short-term disability.

All regular employees shall be permitted to cash out unused sick time while they are out on short-term disability in order to become whole for their normal salary; an employee may use a half sick day while on disability in order to supplement their income and receive one hundred percent pay while on disability.

Section 4: Reimbursement for Prescription Drug Expenses

Upon request, CIR shall make an advance against an employee’s salary during the period of waiting for the reimbursement for prescription expenses.

Section 5: Retiree Health Benefit Coverage

An employee who retires at 65 years of age or higher with at least ten (10) years of service shall receive $1,250.00 per year to be used toward the purchase of personal health benefit coverage.

An employee who retires between the ages of 55 and 65, who is eligible for an SEIU Pension, and whose years of service when added to his/her age totals 80 or more shall receive $500 per year to be used toward the purchase of personal health benefit coverage.

An employee who retires on a SEIU disability pension, regardless of age, shall receive $500 per year to be used for the purchase of personal health benefit coverage.

An employee who retires on a SEIU disability pension, regardless of age will have their COBRA payments paid for by the employer for a maximum of eighteen (18) months.

ARTICLE XIV: GRIEVANCE PROCEDURE

Section 1: Definitions and Steps

All complaints, disputes, or grievances arising between the parties hereto involving questions of interpretation or application of any provisions of Agreement or questions of interpretation or application of any written CIR employment policy shall be handled in the following manner. Nothing herein shall prohibit an employee from first discussing the matter with their immediate supervisor in an effort to resolve the matter prior to filing at Step 1.

Step 1:

The grievance shall be submitted in writing within fifteen (15) business days of its occurrence to the employee’s supervisor. The supervisor shall contact the union to schedule a meeting to discuss the grievance. The meeting must take place within fifteen (15) business days of submission. If the parties do not reach an agreement to settle the grievance at Step 1, the supervisor shall issue a written response within five (5) business days of the meeting.

Step 2:

If the response of the supervisor is unsatisfactory, the grievance shall be submitted to the Executive Director, or designee, within ten (10) business days of the response or when the response was due. A meeting to discuss the grievance will be scheduled within ten (10) business days of submission to Step 2. If the employer and the union do not reach agreement to settle the grievance, the Executive Director or designee shall issue a written response within five (5) business days of the meeting.

Step 3:

If the response of the Executive Director or designee is unsatisfactory, the Union may submit the grievance to final and binding impartial arbitration pursuant to the voluntary rules of the American Arbitration Association (AAA). Such submission shall occur within fifteen (15) business days of the response or, if no timely written response is issued, within fifteen (15) business days from when the response was due. The Employer and the Union shall share the costs of the arbitration equally.

Section 2: General Provisions

All time limits stipulated herein can be extended by mutual agreement of the parties. Requests for extension by either party shall not be unreasonably denied and shall be provided for in writing.

Upon written request by the union, the employer shall provide information in its possession to which the union is entitled to properly represent the employee. Likewise, upon written request, the union shall make available to management any requested information necessary for management to respond to the grievance.

A grievance may be initiated at or moved to any step of the procedure without a meeting or hearing at a lower step by mutual agreement of the parties.

Any grievance that is timely filed by the union at any step of the process may be moved to the next step if the employer does not issue a timely response provided for in the process or extension provided for in writing.

Any grievance that is not moved to the next step in a timely manner by the union in accordance with this process shall be considered withdrawn.

Upon approval of the appropriate supervisor, one CWA representative shall be released for a reasonable period of time from his/her regular duties without loss of pay to attend step 1 and step 2 grievance meetings, arbitration hearings and to represent employees involved in disciplinary proceedings.

If requested by either party, a reasonable number of employees shall be released for a reasonable period of time from his/her regular duties without loss of pay to be a witness at any step 1 and step 2 grievance meetings, arbitration hearings, or disciplinary meeting.

No claims may be presented in arbitration that was not grieved in previous steps of the procedure. Only the union or the employer may submit a grievance to arbitration.

A claim for monies due must be submitted as a grievance no later than thirty (30) days after the time of the alleged violation or no later than thirty (30) days after it was discovered or could reasonably have been known and grieved.

ARTICLE XV: DISCIPLINARY PROCEDURE

Section 1:

All discipline is subject to the just cause standard and should be in accordance with the requirements of the progressive discipline. When the employer intends to impose discipline, an employee should receive a notice of disciplinary action containing the following information: - (a) charges, - (b) brief description of specifications on which the charge is based; - (c) the penalty to be imposed. A copy of the notice of discipline shall be provided to the union.

Section 2:

Upon receipt of the notice of discipline, the employee or union may appeal by filing a grievance within fifteen (15) business days in accordance with the provisions of Article XIV. A notice of discipline for a warning (verbal, written, final) and suspension without pay for five (5) days or less shall be initiated at Step 1. Any notice of discipline for suspension without pay of more than five (5) days, as well as termination, shall be initiated at Step 2.

Section 3:

Except in cases where the proposed discipline is a warning (verbal, written, final) where timely appeals are filed, the employer may not implement any proposed discipline until the expiration of time for filing for arbitration except as noted in Section 4.

Section 4:

An employee may be suspended or terminated immediately upon receipt of notice of discipline where it is determined that the employee has engaged in gross misconduct or is formally charged with a crime of the first, second or third degree. Where a suspension or termination is immediate, the employee shall be notified of the charges and the basis of the immediate suspension. The notice of discipline shall set forth the charges and the basis of the immediate suspension or termination.

Gross misconduct includes deliberate, intentional or willful acts that cannot be justified in any reasonable way or addressed through progressive discipline. Examples of gross misconduct include, but are not limited to: - dishonesty, - falsification of records, - theft, - willful neglect of duty, - insubordination, - being under the influence of drugs or alcohol, - physical assault, and - the willful damage of property.

The employee or the union

The employee or the union, upon receipt of a notice that an employee is being suspended or terminated immediately may, within five (5) business days of the date of receipt of the notice, request a meeting with the Executive Director or designee to discuss the suspension or termination. The meeting will be scheduled within ten (10) business days of the request. If a resolution is not reached at the meeting, the union shall have ten (10) business days from the date of the meeting to file for arbitration pursuant to Step 3 of the grievance procedure.

Section 5

Any record of disciplinary action resulting from lateness or absenteeism imposed on any employee shall be removed from an employee’s file after six (6) months. Any record of disciplinary action resulting from an offense other than lateness, absenteeism, or gross misconduct shall be removed from an employee’s file after two (2) years provided that the employee has not been further disciplined, for any reason during the two (2) year period.

ARTICLE XVI: LABOR/MANAGEMENT MEETINGS

Labor management meetings will take place monthly or upon request from either party to this Agreement. Agenda items to be discussed must be submitted to the other side no less than twenty-four (24) hours in advance of the labor management meeting. Up to six (6) employees who have been appointed or elected as a Union steward or who is affected by an agenda item that was submitted in advance of the labor management meeting shall be released from their regular duties to participate in labor/management meetings and to attend collective bargaining sessions when they are scheduled in whole or in part during regular work hours. Approval of release time shall not be unreasonably denied. The above cap of six (6) does not preclude other members of the bargaining unit whose work schedule allows to attend.

The impact of any such change upon working conditions shall be the subject of negotiations in advance between the employer and the union.

Except as may be specifically limited elsewhere in this Agreement, the employer has the right to make and implement policy; manage the affairs of the organization; assign, direct, and regulate employees; determine the type and number of staff positions and the quality of work required from employees; and shall retain all rights and prerogatives which it possessed at the effective date of this Agreement.

The employer shall continue to make reasonable provisions for the safety and health of its employees during working hours. ARTICLE XXIII: EVALUATIONS AND ACCESS TO PERSONNEL FILES

Evaluations are not disciplinary, nor are they substitute for progressive discipline. Evaluations are confidential employment records.

Employees shall be permitted to challenge the validity of their evaluations in their files through Step 2 of the grievance procedure in which the outcome will be final and binding.

Upon request, staff will be permitted to receive copies of all the documents in their files.

ARTICLE XXIV: PROHIBITION AGAINST DISCRIMINATION

Neither the employer nor the union shall discriminate or harass any employee based on a person’s actual or perceived race, religion, creed, color, age, sexual orientation or preference, prior conviction record, national origin, marital status, gender, gender identity, gender expression, genetic information, carrier status, alienage or citizenship status, ancestry, medical condition, disability, or status as a Vietnam era or special disabled veteran, handicap, veteran status, or any other characteristic protected by federal, state or local law. Affirmative action will be a factor in promotions as permitted by law.

ARTICLE XXV: HOURLY EMPLOYEE PARTICIPATION IN UNION ACTIVITIES

Any hourly employee who is duly appointed as a steward or representative of the union shall be allotted a period of up to three (3) hours per month to participate in labor/management meetings. Their supervisor will be given advance notice.

Meal allowance for employees shall be governed by the employer’s Expense Reimbursement policy.

The employer will provide payroll deductions for the staff-appointed credit union.

The parties shall agree to an affirmative action program for attracting professional staff and devise a timetable for achieving the goals adopted.

In the event of theft or vandalization while employees are engaged in work-related activities, Employees that file a timely police report will not be responsible for the reimbursement or repair cost of equipment provided by the employer (including, but not limited to, cell phones, laptops, and other electronic devices) provided that the employee has exercised due care in the transport and handling of such property.

Employees with appropriate insurance shall submit a claim when equipment provided by the employer is stolen or vandalized. Any amount paid to the employee for the employer’s property as a result of filing an insurance claim shall be turned over to the employer.

Section 7: Outside employment

No employee may work for or represent other unions without prior written consent from their employer. Outside work shall not be done during office hours except during authorized leave. Outside work of any kind is prohibited if it interferes with the work of the employer.

CIR will reimburse all full-time employees for their childcare expenses, that qualify as tax-exempt per IRS regulations, up to $300 per month, for a child from the age of infant to fifteen years of age, upon submission of a receipt. CIR will consider reimbursement of other extraordinary childcare expenses on a case-by-case basis.

Section 9: Identification of Employee’s Supervisor

CIR will inform each employee of their supervisor or supervisors upon their hiring and whenever there is a change. If an employee has more than one supervisor, that employee will be told which supervisor is primary.

Section 10: Union Use of Office Space

The union shall be permitted to use the employer’s office space if available during non-office hours. Such office use shall be scheduled in advance with the Office Manager.

Section 11: Timely Expense Reimbursement

Upon receiving expense reimbursement reports from employees, supervisors will approve the report and send it to the Finance Department or, if edits are needed, will inform the employee of necessary edits within five (5) business days of receiving the report. Reimbursement will be issued within forty-five (45) days of the accounting department receiving the supervisor approved report.

The employer will reimburse expenses submitted more than forty-five (45) calendar days after the month in which the expenses were incurred.

Section 12: IRS 125 Account

CIR will establish an IRS 125 account for dependent and elder care.

Section 13: Pay Day

All Employees will receive their pay in the form of direct deposit every other Friday. Pay stubs shall be provided through ADP or similar service.

ARTICLE XXVII: NO STRIKE, NO LOCKOUT

Neither the Union nor any employee will cause, engage, or participate in any strike, work stoppage, work interruption or sympathy strike. The Employer will not directly or indirectly cause, engage, or participate in any lockout during the life of this Agreement as a result of any dispute with the Union or any employee(s).

ARTICLE XXVIII: SAVING CLAUSE

If any article or section of this agreement or any addendum hereto shall be held to be invalid by operation of law or by any tribunal of competent jurisdiction, or if compliance with an enforcement of any article, section, or addendum should be restricted by such tribunal, the remainder of this Agreement and addenda shall not be affected thereby, and the parties shall enter into immediate collective negotiations for the purpose of arriving at a mutually satisfactory replacement for such article, section or addendum.

ARTICLE XXIX: AGREEMENT TERMINATION AND RENEWAL

This Agreement shall be in full force and effect from February 1, 2022, through and including January 31, 2025 and shall continue in effect and be automatically renewed from year to year unless either party gives written notice to the other, as required by law, of its desire to terminate or modify this Agreement 90 days prior to its termination or anniversary dates.
